turn 1 · one direction

The page is grouped by the wrong thing. Person · country is how the rows were entered; year is how you think about them. 2024 currently appears in three separate places on this page, so “what did 2024 cost me” is a question the layout cannot answer — and it gets worse every year, because a new statement adds a card rather than filling a cell.

the list is a matrix Thirteen statements, four group headings, ~1 400px of scroll. It is really 8 years × 4 jurisdictions — eight rows. Ten more years of filing adds ten rows instead of forty cards.
six charts, one question Gross and tax are drawn once per currency, so no chart shows a year's total and none can be compared to another. Six panels, and the one that answers something — effective rate — is the smallest.
the conversion already exists Nothing was missing: convertOrFace is already imported on this route for the payslip prefill. taxSeries is pure and keys on person|country|currency, so this is a new function beside it that takes the same convert callback payslipYearTotalConverted already receives, and keys on year.
moves read as pay cuts Every jurisdiction's line stops when you left the country, so the charts show four short falling lines and no career. Stacked in one currency, the same data shows income rising 4.7× since 2018.
two buttons per row Twenty-six Edit and Delete buttons, permanently, for a record touched once a year. Delete especially: a filed tax statement is not something to put one misclick away.
a real data bug, visible PL 2025 says gross 368 PLN and 2024 155.07 PLN — annual salaries of €86 and €36. The old layout has nowhere to say that; a matrix makes it the one flagged cell in the grid.

{{ flagged }} PL 2024 and 2025 are two orders of magnitude below every other filing. The flag is on the cell, where the number is — not in a note at the bottom of a card you have to scroll to.
year is the row Eight rows instead of thirteen cards, and it stays eight-ish forever. A year with one filing is one line; a year with three is still one line. The statement cards still exist — they are what a row opens into.
converted to compare, native to record Cells and the expanded statement show exactly what was filed, in its own currency — that is the legal record and must not be rounded through a rate. Totals and the chart convert. The rule the handoff already states, applied here.
the Excel questions, answered Your Income/Taxs and Total tax percentage sheets are the two chart modes. Delta per annum is the green figure in the band rather than a chart — a single year-on-year number does not need 300px.
jurisdiction colours Four pastel steps of --series-* hues — periwinkle, sage, apricot, dusty rose — and deliberately out of the traffic-light hues, which have to keep meaning good/watch/bad. Three jurisdictions keep the family you already read: DE stays on --series-income, ES on -bills, PL on -taxes. CZ is the one that changes — off --series-housing, whose deep teal-blue has no warm pastel, onto --series-health. It is still the set's cool anchor, so the blue/green/warm reading survives; it is a different blue. See the card below for the tokens and the one thing about them still unmeasured.
the chart is house style Taken from LoanSchedule.svelte and TaxCharts.svelte: one rx 2 stacked bar per slot at min(58, slot × 0.5), axis ink in --fg3, baseline --bd2, gridlines --bd, 2px strokes, r 3.5 dots. Five departures, stated. The fill: LoanSchedule uses opaque var(--orange) and var(--green) with no stroke; these bars are a gradient of one hue at stop-opacity 0.62 → 0.42 behind a 1px border of the same hue at full strength — the pill and tile treatment, because at full saturation the bars were the loudest thing on a page whose washes sit at 0.07. The pastel step: four new tokens — --series-health-soft, -income-soft, -bills-soft, -taxes-soft — each a lighter, less saturated step of its parent hue, the same relationship the palette already has between its dark and light values. Periwinkle, sage, apricot, dusty rose. They must be added to app.css; nothing here paints a raw hex. Czechia takes --series-health rather than --series-housing because a deep cold teal-blue has no warm pastel — it lightens to ice. Each soft step holds its parent's hue angle, so hue separation carries over; the lightness separation does not, and has not been re-measured for colour-vision deficiency — that is open, not done. The hatch: that leaves two kinds of money in one hue, so a per-jurisdiction <pattern> separates tax from kept without spending a second colour on it. The labels: 10px HTML spans over the plot, not SVG <text> at --text-2xs, because an interpolated value inside SVG text renders nothing. The readout: per column rather than a <title> per mark — Plotly's hovermode: x unified, which names every jurisdiction in the year beside its own swatch instead of only the one under the cursor. The strip: the rate sits under a shared x axis rather than on a second y axis, because a dual axis is a chart asking to be misread.
