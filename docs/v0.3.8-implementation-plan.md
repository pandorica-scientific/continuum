# v0.3.8 — Statement ingest

Branch `feat/v0.3.8-bank-import`. Written after probing sixteen real and synthetic
statements from nine institutions; the evidence behind every decision here is in
`scratch-workspace/probe/` and the findings report.

**Revised 2026-08-17** after three independent audits — two agents and an external
Codex review — measured this branch against the claims below. Roughly a third of them
were false. Every status line in this document is now backed by a probe or a line
reference, and the sections that were aspirational are marked as such rather than
described in the past tense.

**Updated 2026-08-18.** Phase C is largely landed. What follows records the state
before it; the summary immediately below records what changed and what the numbers
are now. `npm test` (1213 passing), `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npm run lint` are green.

Two defects were found during the work that no audit had identified, and both were
larger than anything on the list:

- **The generic reader split CSV on newlines before parsing quotes.** A newline inside
  a quoted field — a wrapped payment note, a two-line merchant address — cut one
  movement into two rows carrying neither a date nor an amount. `csvLines` had solved
  this and the five adapters had always used it; `grid.ts` never did. Sixty files in
  the synthetic corpus fragmented, recovered a fraction of their rows, and were refused.
- **The amount/balance differences test was specified in Phase 4 and never built.**
  "If one column's consecutive differences equal another column's values, both are
  identified at once" is what makes a headerless ledger readable, and without it every
  headerless export was refused with "nothing in the statement could be checked" while
  its own last column proved every movement in it. Now implemented as
  `pairAmountWithBalance`, over both row orders and both decimal conventions.

Two fixture defects surfaced the moment arithmetic became mandatory, which is the
clearest evidence the gate works: `tests/fixtures/fio.csv` stated a debit total of
50 050 against rows totalling 40 050, and the `fioStatement` generator printed opening
and closing balances of 0,00 while emitting a real movement. Both are corrected.

One proof bug was fixed along the way: `testChain` reported an **untestable** chain as
a **failed** one. A single movement with a single balance and no opening figure is not
a broken chain, and calling it "the running balance does not follow from the movements"
said something false about a consistent file.

### Phase C item 14 — the rhythm assembler, as landed

`src/lib/server/import/tabular/rhythm.ts`. Both assemblers now produce candidate
readings for every PDF and the proof engine chooses between them, which is the design
the comparison study argued for.

Integration took five changes beyond the port itself, each found by measurement:

1. **Repeated values get their own column slot.** Prose wraps and values do not: joining
   a booking date and a value date in one column produced `09.12.2024 08.12.2024`, which
   is not a date, and a 28-record table read as three.
2. **Page furniture is kept.** The assembler drops every line that is not a movement, so
   emitting only movements produced tables with no arithmetic attached — four banks
   assembled perfectly and were refused with "nothing in the statement could be checked",
   the balances having been discarded one step earlier.
3. **Movements are emitted contiguously, furniture after.** A multi-page statement prints
   furniture BETWEEN pages, so interleaving cut one 28-movement table into regions of 7,
   12, 7 and 2.
4. **The column header is recovered by geometry.** It is page furniture by the
   assembler's rule, and without it a `Debit | Credit | Balance` layout has no names and
   the reader guesses one of the pair. Labels are not aligned the way their values are —
   `Debit` sits left of a right-aligned figure — so they are matched on cell midpoints,
   the one position both alignments share. A line carrying both a date and a figure is a
   movement and is never taken as a header.
5. **A short page is carried by the document's rhythm.** Three anchors establish a beat,
   so a final page holding one movement produced no run and contributed nothing: a
   nine-movement statement read as eight and reconciled against nothing. The beat is a
   property of the statement, and by then it is known.

Two further fixes came out of the same work:

- **Evidence is scoped to the assembler that produced it.** Both assemblers read the same
  furniture and extract it differently; sharing across them cost a five-movement
  statement its proof — P4 with every check passing on its own evidence, P0 with the
  other assembler's stated totals.
- **The decimal mark is resolved against the currency's own precision.** "Three trailing
  digits reads equally as a thousands group" is true of a two-decimal currency and false
  of a three-decimal one, so every KWD statement had an undecidable separator and no
  readable amount. The account states the currency, so this is known rather than guessed.

The recorded limitation is deleted from `frompdf.ts` and `corpus.test.ts`. It was wrong
in the way that matters most: it described a defect as a boundary.

### The safety boundary, as landed

The old boundary read the sizes a zip states about itself, which is a defence against
honesty. Measured: a 299 KB upload declaring **0 bytes** of content passed every check,
and SheetJS inflated it anyway — RSS moved 812 MB in 586 ms.

Now the declared sizes are still read, because they give the clearest message when they
are honest, and then every entry is actually inflated under a **shared** byte budget —
shared rather than per-entry, so the total work is bounded however the payload is
divided. Stored entries are charged to the budget without inflating, since their output
is their input and the input is already measured. The walk is time-bounded too.

```
honest 300 MB declaration          299 KB upload   REFUSED
declares 0 bytes, expands 300 MB   299 KB upload   REFUSED   <- previously ACCEPTED
declares 12 KB, expands 300 MB     299 KB upload   REFUSED   <- previously ACCEPTED
an honest small workbook                           ACCEPTED
```

Plus a sheet cap and a per-sheet cell cap, checked against the declared range before any
of it becomes values: bounding the compressed bytes bounds the XML, and the XML is not
what costs the memory. Pinned in `tests/unit/safety-zip.test.ts`.

### C11 — marked rather than deleted, and why

**OCR has still never read a statement.** Re-measured with every fix from this work in
place, across 64 raster derivatives of the synthetic corpus — PNG at 300 dpi, JPEG at
150, TIFF at 400, scanned PDF at 200: **0 read, 64 refused.**

The pipeline is not what fails. The table is found and most rows assemble; the digits are
wrong:

```
812.62    -> 612.62        -411.64   -> -41164   (the decimal is lost)
89.80     -> 1350          28,873.01 -> 28,073.01
29,775.01 -> 239,775.01    -86.11    -> -66.11
```

Substitutions, not formatting, so no post-processing recovers them. Whatever is wrong
sits upstream of the text — rendering, contrast, or the recogniser's configuration — and
has not been found. The half that matters holds: all 64 refused, none imported wrongly.

Both OCR and the profile store are **kept and marked in their own headers with the
measurement**, rather than deleted. The harm the plan named was that unreachable code
_reads as a delivered promise_; a status note removes that harm, and deleting coherent,
isolated work that is real planned scope would only mean writing it again.

What has been removed is the actual promise. The upload dialog no longer offers
`.png/.jpg/.jpeg/.heic`, because a format that always fails is worse than one that is
absent, and its description no longer claims photographs are read. `.tsv` was added,
because it now works.

If the wizard and the queue are dropped from the release, these two should go with them
rather than linger.

## The governing rule

> **No preset may ever be the reason a statement is accepted. A preset can only make
> acceptance _fast_. Acceptance itself is always arithmetic.**

**Status: not delivered.** This is still the right rule and it is still the point of
the release, but the branch does not implement it, and saying otherwise was the single
most misleading claim in the previous draft.

`proveStatement` has exactly **one** call site in the whole codebase — `detect.ts:343`,
inside `readGenerically`. Everything else reaches the ledger without arithmetic:

| Route                                                    | Files                           | Gated? |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------ |
| Generic tabular / PDF reader                             | `detect.ts:343`                 | yes    |
| Five hand-written adapters (fio, revolut, mbank, rb, cs) | `detect.ts:163-173`, `:235-242` | **no** |
| Three standards parsers (camt053, mt940, abo)            | `detect.ts:126-141`             | **no** |

That is not theoretical. Tampering with real samples — deleting one movement from a
statement that prints its own closing balance — is accepted on every ungated route:

```
ACCEPTED  fio, one row deleted       rows=5   endpointGap=2 976 000  autoImport=false
ACCEPTED  mbank, one row deleted     rows=4   endpointGap=30 000     autoImport=false
ACCEPTED  revolut, one row deleted   rows=37  endpointGap=null       autoImport=TRUE
ACCEPTED  mt940, one movement short           endpointGap=-541 590
ACCEPTED  camt.053, 100.00 against a 4000.00 balance change  endpointGap=390 000
```

`autoImport=false` is not a safety net here — nothing asks the user, because the
question flow lives on the generic route too. The only arithmetic check these routes
meet is `assertRowsExplainTheBalance` (`detect.ts:37-46`), which fires only when a
statement has **zero** rows _and_ differing endpoints. One row short of a hundred
sails through.

And the rule is contradicted by the plan's own Phase 4 policy table. `proof.ts:355-360`
returns `autoImport: true` for a verified profile **before** any proof class is
consulted:

```
REFUSED  no profile:                    ... nothing in the statement could be checked
REFUSED  unverified saved profile:      ... nothing in the statement could be checked
ACCEPTED verified saved profile:  rows=3 proof=P0 opening=undefined closing=undefined
```

A P0 statement — no opening balance, no closing balance, no totals, no running balance —
imports because a preset said so. That is the rule's exact counterexample, written into
the policy table at line "P0 → ask, or use a verified saved profile". One of the two has
to go. **The rule stays; the policy line goes.**

(This path is unreachable in production today only because nothing ever writes a
profile — see Phase 5.)

### The two honest qualifications, revisited

- **For tabular formats (CSV/XLSX) generic discovery is strong.** Confirmed and
  unchanged. A reader with no bank knowledge finds mBank's and Revolut's amount and
  balance columns purely by arithmetic, including Revolut's `Amount − Fee` composite.
- **For PDF layouts it was called materially weaker. That claim is now disproven.** The
  limitation written into `frompdf.ts:17-20` and `corpus.test.ts:162-176` — that
  multi-line transaction records are a structural boundary — is **false**, and must be
  deleted rather than softened. A second assembler reads all four "impossible" files,
  each confirmed against its own printed totals. See _Two readers, one arbiter_.

### What genuinely works

Measured, not asserted. Eleven of nineteen corpus samples import, 432 rows in total, and
every one of them agrees with its own arithmetic:

- nine samples close exactly on `opening + Σ(amount − fee) = closing`
- Revolut (38 rows) and CaixaBank (140 rows) print no endpoints and instead carry a
  complete running chain
- zero samples import without some arithmetic agreement

Three PDFs — UK, German, CaixaBank — are read with **no bank-specific code at all**,
by glyph geometry and arithmetic alone. CaixaBank is 140 rows joined across eight pages.
That is the part of the thesis that is proven.

Also genuinely delivered:

- Raiffeisenbank's January template, which read **zero rows** before this branch, now
  reads 43 and reconciles. Regression fixture committed.
- pdf.js detaches the buffer it is handed. `pdftext.ts:10` now passes a copy — before
  this branch **every stored original PDF was written to disk as 0 bytes.** A
  pre-existing production bug, fixed here, worth crediting on its own.
- The five adapters produce byte-identical `ParsedRow[]` to their pre-branch versions on
  every real sample, verified against `4626934`. The only difference is the intended RB
  January fix.
- Nothing calls home. Verified by sealing `fetch`, `http/https.request`, `net.connect`
  and `net.Socket.prototype.connect` and running the full OCR path: **0 outbound
  attempts.** `langPath` is absolute, so tesseract.js takes its local-file branch and
  never reaches the jsdelivr default.
- Macros and formulas are never executed (`bookVBA: false`, `raw: false`).
- A per-file corpus suite exists, naming what each sample must do and why — written
  because four changes in a row each fixed one statement and broke another while the
  aggregate pass count stayed flat.

### What is broken, in severity order

**1. A wrong import happens today.** `KU_3_month_bank_statment.pdf` — CaixaBank, 140
transactions, denominated in euro — imports as **CZK**, at P3, auto-imported, and the
corpus suite is green because it never asserts currency.

```
CaixaBank imports as currency: CZK   rows: 140
first row currency: CZK
€ glyphs in the file: 66
```

The cause is `statement.ts:186-192`. The symbol test matches symbol-_then_-digit, which
is the US and UK order and almost nowhere else in Europe:

```
"600,41 €"      -> NOT DETECTED       "€ 600,41"           -> €
"1 234,56 Kč"   -> NOT DETECTED       "Balance 600.41 EUR" -> EUR
"500 zł"        -> NOT DETECTED
"12 500 Ft"     -> NOT DETECTED
```

Detection fails, and `statement.ts:433` finishes the job:

```ts
const currency = options.currency ?? evidence.currency ?? 'CZK';
```

A default currency is a fabricated fact about someone's money. It must not exist:
undetected currency is a **question**, never a guess.

The root cause is deeper than the regex, and the repair is in _Currency and bank come
from the account_: the importing account already states its currency, so the document
should never have been the source. The regex fix stays worth making — for the
cross-check and for a first import with no account yet — but it stops being what stands
between the user and a wrong number.

**2. Multi-currency rows are stored 100× wrong.** `statement.ts:434` derives minor-unit
digits from the _statement's_ currency while `:477` labels each row with its _own_
currency. A HUF row inside a CZK statement:

```
file says   3 000,00 HUF   (= 3000 minor, HUF is zero-decimal)
stored as  -300000 HUF
proof P3, autoImport=true
```

Deriving digits per row is the wrong repair. An account has **one** ledger currency —
`account.currency` is a single column and `resolveAccount` refuses a mismatch — so a HUF
line inside a CZK statement is a CZK movement with a foreign original, not a HUF
movement. Fixed properly by the same change: _Currency and bank come from the account_.

**3. A transaction whose description contains a summary word is silently deleted.**
`regions.ts:169-176` filters rows by `looksLikeSummary` _before_ `readTabular` counts
them, so the drop is invisible to the question logic. Reproduced: 3 rows in, 2 rows
stored, 0 questions asked, P3, `autoImport=true`.

There is a nuance neither review caught. In production that fixture is currently
_refused_ — but for the wrong reason. `detect.ts:285` gathers evidence across **all**
candidate readings (`candidates.flatMap((grid) => detectRegions(grid))`), so one
reading's balances are used to judge another's rows, manufacturing a spurious
contradiction. **One bug is masking the other.** Fixing the evidence pollution without
fixing the row filter turns a refusal into a wrong import.

**4. P3 with no printed opening balance proves nothing about the head of the file.**
`testChain` (`proof.ts:105-108`) anchors to an opening balance only when one exists.
Revolut and CaixaBank print none, so deleting their _first_ row leaves the chain closing
perfectly. That is 178 of the corpus's 432 imported rows, auto-imported on an unanchored
chain.

**5. The decompression-bomb gate reads attacker-controlled numbers.**
`assertSafeToParse` (`safety.ts:100-149`) trusts the zip **central directory**:

```
honest 240 MB expansion, ratio 1029       -> REFUSED (correctly)
directory declares 0 bytes uncompressed   -> ACCEPTED
directory declares 12 KB per entry        -> ACCEPTED
```

And SheetJS inflates it regardless: a **299 KB upload moved RSS by 812 MB in 586 ms**.
The declared sizes are a courtesy, not a bound. Also missing entirely: sheet, row,
column and cell caps, a processing-time ceiling and a memory ceiling — all promised in
Phase 1, none implemented. Zip inspection covers only `xlsx`/`ods`; a PDF gets a 32 MB
length check and nothing else.

**6. A workbook with one sheet per account loses accounts silently.**
`readGenerically` (`detect.ts:294-299`) sorts candidate readings and returns
`readings[0].statements` — one winner, and for a workbook that is one candidate _per
sheet_:

```
sheets in the workbook: Cuenta 1111, Cuenta 2222
statements returned: 1   (rows=3)
```

The second account's movements vanish, `totalRows > 0` so ingest proceeds, and the
content hash is recorded — so the corrected re-import is later refused as a duplicate.
That is precisely the failure mode this release exists to eliminate, reintroduced in a
new place. Arbitrating candidate readings by `max(rowCount)` is wrong in principle:
**disagreement between readings is a question, not a contest.**

**7. OCR has never read a statement.** 15 attempts across every ground-truth render and
all five refused PDFs: **15 refusals, 0 statements.** The German page that reaches P4
from its text layer reaches **P0** from pixels. Nothing sets `options.ocr: true`
anywhere in `src/`, so the path is unreachable in production regardless, and no test in
`tests/` exercises it. The image formats advertised in the upload dialog
(`.png,.jpg,.jpeg,.heic`) therefore always fail. The safe half holds — it degrades to a
refusal, never to a wrong import — but "arbitration" is unearned.

**8. Phase 5 is unreachable code.** `saveProfile`, `profileFromReading`,
`saveDriftedProfile`, `exportProfile`, `importProfileDocument`, `deleteProfile` and
`recordProfileUse` have **zero callers** in `src/`. Only `loadProfiles` is called.
`BUILTIN_PROFILES` is `[]`. There is no wizard and no drift dialog — `src/routes/(app)/import/`
holds a `+page.server.ts` and a `+page.svelte` and nothing else. `import_profile` is
always empty in production, which is the only reason defect (1)'s verified-profile
bypass has not fired.

**9. Two geometric bugs with named line numbers.** `frompdf.ts:235` compares against
`previous.y`, which is never advanced when lines are absorbed (`:238-243` pushes cells,
xs and xEnds but not y), so the absorption window drifts. `regions.ts:96-102` splits
regions on exact populated width, which is fatal for ragged geometry-derived grids.

## Phase C — Consolidation (next, and before any new parsing)

The branch has more reading ability than it has safety. Every item here closes a hole
that is open right now; none of it adds a bank.

1. **One gate, no exceptions.** Route the adapters and the standards parsers through
   `proveStatement`/`decideImport`. A trusted mapping may skip _discovery_; it may not
   skip _arithmetic_. Where a parser produces a statement whose own printed balances
   disagree with its rows, that is a question, not an import.
2. **Move the `verifiedProfile` branch below the proof-class check** (`proof.ts:355`)
   and delete the "or use a verified saved profile" line from the P0 policy.

### Phase 4 — The proof engine — **built, not wired**

The engine is sound and the design below stands. Its problem is reach, not correctness.

**Ledger models are enumerated hypotheses, never assumptions:** signed amount column;
`credit − debit` **and** `debit − credit` pairs; amount net of a separate fee
(`Amount − Fee`, Revolut); ascending **and** descending row order; balance-after /
balance-before / available / ledger.

**Proof classes, ranked lexicographically** — proof class first, heuristic score only
ever breaks ties:

|        | Evidence                                                                                       |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **P4** | running chain + opening + closing + stated totals + row count                                  |
| **P3** | complete running chain only — **must be anchored to a printed opening balance to auto-import** |
| **P2** | aggregate closure; stronger when independent debit **and** credit totals agree                 |
| **P1** | endpoints only                                                                                 |
| **P0** | no arithmetic evidence                                                                         |

**Lexical constraints gate every class.** Arithmetic closure alone is not proof: a
uniform 1000× misreading of amounts _and_ balances closes the chain perfectly. Require
consistent decimal and grouping, monetary precision compatible with the currency,
displayed text agreeing with the inferred scale, identifier columns never reinterpreted
as money, and consistent negative markers.

**Sign is derived from the balance movement wherever a balance column exists**; a
printed `S`/`H`/`−`/`( )` is corroboration only. Evidence: OCR read `87,45 S` as
`87,45 Ss` and a strict match dropped the whole row while the amount itself was perfect.
One glyph must never decide a transaction's direction.

**Auto-import policy** (revised — the P0 escape hatch is gone):

```
P4 / P3-anchored + lexical checks pass   -> import silently
P2 / P1 + every dimension determined     -> import silently
P2 / P1 + one dimension undetermined     -> ask about THAT DIMENSION ONLY
P3-unanchored                            -> ask
P0                                       -> ask
```

Determinacy tests, all proven against real files: **date order** — any component > 12
settles it, else the stated period settles it (the US fixture has 14 dates all ≤ 12 and
is resolved only by "March 1, 2025 through March 31, 2025"), else ask; monotonicity is
not evidence, because banks post in operational order. **Decimal separator** — a value
carrying both settles it; a 3-digit group beside 2-digit groups settles it. **Amount vs
balance** — if one column's consecutive differences equal another column's values, both
are identified at once and the file promotes to a chain proof.

## Currency and bank come from the account

A statement is never read in a vacuum. It is imported **into an account**, and that
account was created by the user, who stated its currency and its bank. That metadata is
the authority. The document is corroboration — exactly as a printed `S`/`H` marker
corroborates a sign that the balance movement decides.

This inverts the current flow, which guesses first and validates second:

```
today:     parse -> guess currency -> resolve account -> compare -> blame the user
corrected: account -> currency -> parse -> compare with the document -> ask on conflict
```

Most of this already exists and is simply not connected:

- `statement.ts:433` reads `options.currency ?? evidence.currency ?? 'CZK'`. The account's
  currency belongs in that first slot. Today only a saved profile ever fills it
  (`detect.ts:328`).
- `resolveAccount` (`ingest.ts:139-149`) already refuses when `chosen.currency` or
  `chosen.bank` disagrees with the statement. It is a good check pointed the wrong way:
  it currently adjudicates our own fabricated guess.
- `+page.svelte:27` already sends `accountId`.

What this fixes at the root, rather than papering over:

- **The live wrong import.** CaixaBank into a euro account becomes EUR because the
  account says EUR. No symbol regex is on the critical path. (The bidirectional symbol
  fix is still worth making — for the cross-check, and for a first import that has no
  account yet — but it stops being load-bearing.)
- **The 100× multi-currency bug.** `ParsedRow` already encodes the correct model
  (`types.ts:24-26`):

  ```ts
  /** Original amount for FX card payments billed in the account currency. */
  originalAmountMinor?: bigint;
  originalCurrency?: string;
  ```

  The ledger amount is in the account's currency and the foreign original is a separate
  pair of fields. `statement.ts:477` breaks that contract by writing a row's foreign
  currency into `currency`. The defect is field confusion, not a missing per-row digit
  rule — the generic reader must populate `originalCurrency`/`originalAmountMinor` and
  leave `currency` as the account's. Multi-currency Revolut pockets are separate
  accounts, which is already how the system models them.

- **Bank routing.** A known account names its bank, so the adapter is selected rather
  than sniffed. This does not violate the governing rule: choosing a reader is
  _discovery_, and discovery may be fast. Acceptance stays arithmetic.

Two constraints this introduces, both real:

1. **Order of operations.** Currency determines minor-unit digits, which determine the
   parsed integers. When the user picks the account at upload, currency is known before
   parsing and everything is simple. When the account is auto-resolved by account number
   — which happens _after_ parsing — amounts must remain lossless until the account is
   known, and be scaled once. This is the plan's own "extract as strings; never coerce
   during extraction" rule, not yet carried through to the amount scale.
2. **The first import for a new account.** There is no account metadata yet, so the
   document is the only source. This is precisely where a **question** belongs — the
   wizard confirms currency and bank while creating the account — and precisely where a
   guess is least acceptable, because the guess becomes permanent metadata that every
   later import is validated against.

Conflict handling: when the account says EUR and the document plainly says CZK, that is
not a parse failure and not a silent override. It is a genuine disagreement about which
account this statement belongs to, and it is a question — which `resolveAccount` already
words well, once it is comparing a _read_ currency instead of a _manufactured_ one.

## Two readers, one arbiter

The multi-line question is settled: **it was never a structural limit.** A second
assembler — prototype in `scratch-workspace/probe/rhythm.ts` — reads every file the
current reader refuses.

It works by never classifying a line. Instead it asks what the page's record _beat_ is:
cluster cells into columns (left edge for text, right edge for figures), find
type-pure regularly spaced recurrences inside one column, treat each such run as a
hypothesis about the beat, and let content **hang below** its anchor — band _i_ is
(anchor _i+1_, anchor _i_]. A hypothesis is valid when the table it implies has one
figure per record in a money column and at least one date per record; among valid
hypotheses, the one accounting for the most cells wins. Nickel is the case that proves
the design: its row-number column implies an invalid table, its amount column implies a
valid one, and no per-layout switch is needed — the wrong anchor simply fails
validation. Results are stable across column tolerance 3–12 pt.

Two other approaches were measured and rejected on the real data, which is worth
recording so they are not retried: whitespace-band segmentation (no threshold exists —
Sparkasse separates records by 14 pt and their own lines by 12 pt, and Nickel vertically
_centres_ rows so intra-record gaps run smaller than inter-record ones), and template
induction with sequence labelling (breaks on Nickel, where the line carrying the date is
not the line carrying the amount).

columns, so "one rhythm per document" keeps only the largest.

### The consequence for the architecture

This is the outcome to build on, and it is better than a winner would have been. Both
assemblers become **candidate readings**, and the proof engine selects. In every case
where they disagree, arithmetic picks the right one without knowing which reader
produced it:

- CaixaBank — 140 completes the running chain, 132 reaches 97%. Chain proof wins.
- Raiffeisenbank January — 43 rows reconcile to the printed closing balance, 44 do not.
  Endpoint proof wins.

That is the governing rule doing exactly the job it was written for, on the one
experiment designed to break it. It only works once **`max(rowCount)` arbitration is
gone** (Phase C item 8) — under the current rule CaixaBank is fine but Raiffeisenbank
would silently pick the wrong reading, because 44 > 43.

Adding the rhythm assembler is therefore **Phase C item 13, and strictly after items 1–8**.
A second reader before the gate is universal is a second way to be confidently wrong.

### One production bug found by the prototype

`isDateLike` (`determinacy.ts:26`) has no range check on the components, so on any

"88.40"  true    "120.00" true    "45.00"  true
"1.83"   true    "377.93" true    "1234.56" true

The comment directly above it claims the opposite ("anchored at both ends so a plain

## The synthetic corpus — the first honest generalisation test

`scratch-workspace/synthetic-test/` (generated with Codex, gitignored): **486 files, 60
statements, 24 locales, 20 currencies, 16 archetypes**, each statement emitted in up to
10 formats. Ground truth gives exact per-row minor amounts, balances, fees and FX
originals. Its arithmetic is sound — 57 of 60 reconcile on endpoints _and_ carry a
complete running chain; the 3 exceptions are dormant-account statements that correctly
print no balances.

Its archetypes map onto our failure taxonomy deliberately: `rhythm-multiline`,
`foreign-amount`, `competing-tables`, `multi-sheet-workbook`, `concatenated-statements`,
`suffix-direction`, `split-direction`, `reverse-chronological`, `headerless-ledger`,
`metadata-csv`. It also carries the minor-unit cases our real corpus lacks entirely:
**KWD (three decimals)** and **JPY (zero)**.

Baseline of the current reader, scored on the ledger-meaningful quantity
(`amount − fee`), not raw `amountMinor` — the ground truth stores fees separately and we
fold them in, which is a representation difference, not an error:

```
FORMAT      files  read  refused  rows-exact  ccy-ok  amounts-exact
csv          120    60       60           3       0              3
tsv           60     0       60           0       0              0
pdf-text      57     0       57           0       0              0
camt053       19    19        0          19      19             19
mt940         15    15        0          15      15             15
abo            3     3        0           3       3              3
ofx           10     0       10           0       0              0
qif            8     0        8           0       0              0
xlsx           1     1        0           0       0              0
ods            1     0        1           0       0              0
```

**The three standards parsers are perfect: 37/37 files, every row, every amount, every
currency**, across all 24 locales including KWD's three decimals. That is the strongest
evidence in the release, and it is evidence for the _format-first_ thesis: where a
document states its own structure, no heuristics are needed and nothing goes wrong.

**Everything discovered rather than declared fails.** Real-corpus performance (11 of 19)
did not generalise, because the real corpus is what the reader was developed against.
This is the number to manage the release by.

Four distinct causes, none of them "PDFs are hard":

1. **Adapter hijack — 60 files.** The synthetic CSVs use mBank's `#`-prefixed header
   convention, so the mBank sniffer claims every comma-delimited file and then dies with
   `mBank: transaction header not found`. An adapter that cannot parse a file it claimed
   must **fall through to the generic reader**, never abort the import. This is the
   adapter routing being treated as a decision instead of a hypothesis — the governing
   rule's exact shape, one layer up.
2. **Silent zero-row reads — 57 files.** Every semicolon/win1250 CSV returns a statement
   with **zero rows and no complaint**. `got − want` equals `−rowcount` exactly. This is
   the RB January bug, reproduced 57 times. Production's `ingest.ts:517` catches it
   before the content hash is recorded, so these are refusals and not corrupt imports —
   but the engine must not report success on an empty read.
3. **Currency read from a metadata label — 60 files.** The generic reader returns
   `#Numer rachunku` — a Polish column heading for "account number" — as the ISO currency
   code. Worse than the `'CZK'` default, and further evidence for taking currency from
   the account (see above) rather than from the document.
4. **PDF fragmentation — 57 files.** Every synthetic PDF is refused, and the message is
   specific: `3 of 8 rows`, `2 of 5 rows`. The reader finds the table and recovers a
   third of it. This is precisely what the rhythm assembler fixes, and it refuses rather
   than importing a third of a statement — the proof gate doing its job.

Unsupported by design and correctly refused: OFX (10), QIF (8), ODS (1).

Two smaller findings: 4 files are refused for an undecidable decimal separator (`every
comma is…`, `every dot is…`), which is the determinacy engine working as intended; and
TSV is read as a grid but yields no evidence on 55 of 60, suggesting tab is not reaching
the delimiter candidates.

**This corpus should become the acceptance suite** once Phase C lands — it is larger than
the real corpus by a factor of twenty-five, it is safe to commit (no real financial
data), and unlike `bank_data_examples_do_not_share/` it can live in CI.

## Open risks

- **The safety boundary trusts attacker-supplied numbers.** Highest-severity open item
  after the wrong-currency import.
- **Unanchored P3 cannot see a movement dropped from the head of the file** — the
  Revolut and CaixaBank case, 178 of 432 imported rows.
- **A workbook silently drops every sheet but one**, and records the content hash, so
  the corrected re-import is refused as a duplicate.
- **Tesseract loses summary blocks** while reading transaction tables well, so stated
  totals may be unavailable on scans even where the format prints them. The chain is the
  dependable oracle there — which makes anchoring it (Phase C item 7) more important, not
  less.
- **Image quality floor** — no rendered page has yet reconciled at any quality. The floor
  is not "300 dpi"; it is currently unknown, and must be measured before the image
  formats stay in the upload dialog.
- **Unreachable code that looks finished.** OCR and the profile store both read as
  delivered features in a code review and are neither. This is how the previous draft of
  this document came to be a third wrong.
